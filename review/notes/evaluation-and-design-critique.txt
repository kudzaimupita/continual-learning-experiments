##### evaluation-and-design-critique: 24 findings
[0] (confirmed; conf high) The standard continual-learning metrics are average accuracy after the final task, backward transfer (final accuracy on a task minus accuracy right after learning it; large negative values are catastrophic forgetting) and forward transfer (effect on tasks not yet trained). The founder's tables already contain the accuracy matrix needed to report BWT per round rather than only raw counts.
   EV: GEM defines ACC, BWT and FWT over a T x T accuracy matrix; it states that large negative backward transfer is what is known as catastrophic forgetting.
   SRC: Gradient Episodic Memory for Continual Learning | Lopez-Paz, Ranzato (Facebook AI Research) | 2017 | https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.08840
[1] (confirmed; conf high) CKL is the foundational benchmark for continual knowledge learning: it separately scores retained time-invariant knowledge, updated knowledge and new knowledge, and summarises the trade-off as FUAR (invariant facts forgotten per fact gained; below 1 is good). LoRA and other parameter-expansion methods forgot far less than plain fine-tuning, forgetting grew with multiple update phases, and no learning-rate rule of thumb was found.
   EV: InvariantLAMA 17,474 / UpdatedLAMA 924 / NewLAMA 797 probes. FUAR: T5-Vanilla 1.08, T5-LoRA 0.55, K-Adapter 0.33, Mix-Review 1.74. InvariantLAMA accuracy fell 11.86 to 9.40 going from one phase to two phases.
   SRC: Towards Continual Knowledge Learning of Language Models | Jang et al. (KAIST, LG AI Research), ICLR 2022 | 2022 | https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.03215
[2] (confirmed; conf high) Benchmarks that include changed facts show closed-book continual learners, LoRA included, do not separate updated from outdated answers, while a retrieval baseline does. The considered design's log needs versioned facts and its evaluation needs an 'outdated answer' score; the founder's made-up towns never change a fact, so this failure mode is untested.
   EV: EvolvingQA (T5-large, Wikipedia Feb-Aug 2023), exact match at the March step, Updated vs Outdated: Full FT 2.23 vs 2.43; LoRA 2.64 vs 2.64; DPR retrieval 23.87 vs 4.23. Results are from a single run.
   SRC: Carpe Diem: On the Evaluation of World Knowledge in Lifelong Language Models (EvolvingQA) | Kim et al. (KAIST), NAACL 2024 | 2023 | https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08106v2
[3] (confirmed; conf high) Incremental training on only the changed data can match full retraining at much lower cost on stream benchmarks, and retrieval gives the fastest adaptation, but a stale underlying model still underperforms a retrained one. This is the main published argument for keeping a parametric path at all.
   EV: TemporalWiki: training on the Wikipedia diff reaches similar or better perplexity than the full snapshot at 12 times less compute. StreamingQA abstract: adding new articles to the search space gives rapid adaptation, and models with an outdated underlying LM under-perform those with a retrained LM.
   SRC: TemporalWiki: A Lifelong Benchmark for Training and Evaluating Ever-Evolving Language Models; StreamingQA (arxiv.org/abs/2205.11388) | Jang et al. (EMNLP 2022); Liska et al., DeepMind (ICML 2022) | 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14211
[4] (confirmed; conf high) At web scale, continual pretraining with fixed-ratio replay matches periodic retraining from scratch at a fraction of the compute, so 'retrain from scratch' is the reference upper bound rather than a necessity; but replay of old data can hurt on fast-changing domains, which matters for a design that replays everything forever.
   EV: TiC-LM: 114 monthly Common Crawl dumps; the seven from-scratch Oracle models cost 1.16T tokens in total versus 220B-440B for continual runs; comparable held-out loss at 2.6x less compute; replay hurt on rapidly evolving domains such as StackOverflow and PyTorch, while non-replay methods stayed competitive on Wikipedia.
   SRC: TiC-LM: A Web-Scale Benchmark for Time-Continual LLM Pretraining | Li et al. (Apple), ACL 2025 | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02107v3
[5] (partly_correct; conf medium) Closest published analogue to the considered design (same model family and size): on a 24-month Wikidata stream, cumulative LoRA replay at higher rank beats a slow/fast adapter hierarchy on retention, including on unseen paraphrases, but conclusions flip with adapter rank and with endpoint versus time-averaged scoring, total replay cost is quadratic in stream length, and accuracy on an untouched control set is well below 1. Endpoint-only, single-rank comparisons (the founder's current protocol) can pick the wrong method.
   EV: 4,739 facts, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B/3B, 3 seeds. Llama-1B: rank-72 replay retention 0.979 (paraphrase 0.947) vs hierarchy 0.885 (0.814). Qwen2.5-1.5B: hierarchy's +5.0 point lead over rank-8 replay becomes an 11.6 point deficit against rank-72 replay. Stability on unchanged control: 0.739 (r8), 0.682 (r72). Ranking flipped in 9 of 24 months; total cost stated as quadratic with raw replay. Base-model stability and whether replay retrains from base are not reported.
   SRC: Beyond Endpoint Scores: Time- and Capacity-Conditioned Evaluation of Continual Knowledge Updating | Heejin Choi (single-author arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed as far as the page shows) | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03900
   CORRECTION: The paper exists (Heejin Choi, single author, v1, 3 Sep 2026) and every number is in it, but the attribution and scope are off. 0.979 / 0.947 vs 0.885 / 0.814 are time-averaged scores on Llama-3.2-1B, not Qwen. The Qwen2.5-1.5B figures are: rank-72 replay 0.984 retention and 0.868 on paraphrases; rank-8 replay 0.818 / 0.669; hierarchy 0.868 / 0.759. Stability 0.739 (r8) and 0.682 (r72) are also Llama-1B; on Qwen they are 0.668 and 0.656. Stability is multiple-choice accuracy on unchanged facts, and no base-model value is reported, so the paper shows it falling with rank on Llama but does not show it is 'reduced' relative to the base. The 9-of-24-month flips are hierarchy vs rank-8 replay only. The stream is one domain dominated by one Wikidata relation (P54), intermediate ranks are single-seed or untested on Qwen, and no general-capability measure (MMLU or similar) is taken. The paper does not say whether replay retrains from base. The quadratic cost is the author's own back-of-envelope accounting, not a measurement.
[6] (partly_correct; conf high) LoRA rank is not the binding constraint for memorising thousands of facts; collateral damage is. A rank-1 LoRA on an 8B model learned 3,000 unknown facts almost perfectly, yet general benchmarks dropped substantially, previously known facts were lost, and the model stopped refusing. Critique: expect the design's general-knowledge gate to fail as the log grows even when retraining from base, unless replay scales with it.
   EV: Llama-3.1-8B, LoRA rank 1: reliability 0.98 at 3,000 unknown facts (1.0 up to 500). MMLU 0.677 baseline fell to 0.615 (10 facts, no mixing), 0.554 (500 facts + 10 known each), 0.441 (3,000 facts + 10 paraphrases). TruthfulQA MC1 0.382 to 0.305. HighlyKnown-to-Unknown shifts: 5 (1 fact) to 409 (10 facts) to 1,134 (500 facts). Base model refused in about 15% of cases, trained models almost never.
   SRC: How Much Knowledge Can You Pack into a LoRA Adapter without Harming LLM? | Pletenev et al. (AIRI, Skoltech) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502v3
   CORRECTION: All numbers check out (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, rank 1, alpha 2, 10 epochs). The conclusion that rank is not the binding constraint goes beyond the paper: only rank 1 was tested, and when 10 HighlyKnown rows were mixed in per fact, reliability at 3,000 facts fell to 0.48 (0.92 with 1 per fact). The authors blame 10 epochs being too few, and do not analyse capacity. The same table also weakens the 'unless replay scales with it' critique: mixing in known facts is the replay-like mitigation, and MMLU still fell to 0.554 with it. The 0.441 figure is the paraphrase-augmented run, so augmentation itself coincided with the largest MMLU loss.
[7] (partly_correct; conf high) Low rank slows and can cap pure memorisation of arbitrary associations; higher rank closes most of the gap to full fine-tuning. For a log expected to reach thousands of facts, rank 8 is likely too low and a rank sweep is required before concluding anything.
   EV: 10K random UUID pairs on LLaMA-2 7B, character accuracy by step: full fine-tuning 100% at step 500; LoRA rank 256 70.6% at 500 and 100% at 700; LoRA rank 8 only 54.2% at step 900.
   SRC: MoRA: High-Rank Updating for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning | Jiang et al. (Beihang University, Microsoft) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12130v1
   CORRECTION: Table numbers are exact (FFT 100 at step 500; LoRA r256 70.6 then 100 at step 700; LoRA r8 54.2 at step 900). The source shows low rank is slower under a fixed step budget, not that it caps: rank 8 was still rising at step 900, and MoRA at rank 8 reached 100. 'Rank 8 is likely too low for thousands of facts' is an extrapolation from random UUID strings, and sits awkwardly beside finding 6, where rank 1 memorised 3,000 natural-language facts.
[8] (confirmed; conf high) LoRA trades learning capacity for less forgetting relative to full fine-tuning: it underperforms full fine-tuning in standard low-rank settings but preserves out-of-domain base ability better than weight decay or dropout; full fine-tuning learns perturbations of much higher rank.
   EV: Abstract: full fine-tuning learns weight perturbations with rank 10-100x greater than typical LoRA configurations; LoRA better maintains base-model performance outside the target domain (code and maths, instruction tuning and continued pretraining).
   SRC: LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less | Biderman et al. (Columbia, Databricks), TMLR 2024 | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673
[9] (confirmed; conf low) Model capacity in bits is unlikely to be the first limit: GPT-style transformers store roughly 3.6 bits per parameter. Extrapolating this to LoRA adapter parameters is my inference, not a result from the paper, so it should be measured (accuracy versus number of facts at fixed rank) rather than assumed.
   EV: Abstract: capacity of approximately 3.6 bits per parameter, measured on hundreds of transformers from 500K to 1.5B parameters trained from scratch (not adapters).
   SRC: How much do language models memorize? | Morris et al. (Meta FAIR, Google DeepMind, Cornell, NVIDIA) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24832
[10] (confirmed; conf high) Stacking LoRA updates is specifically risky, which supports step 3 of the design (retrain from base rather than continue the last adapter): LoRA introduces 'intruder dimensions' absent in full fine-tuning, and sequential training accumulates them, producing more forgetting than full fine-tuning even though a single LoRA round forgets less. This matches the founder's ten-round erosion.
   EV: Quote: "these results show the danger of using LoRA during continual learning". RoBERTa trained sequentially on six tasks; intruder dimensions seen for rank <=16 on RoBERTa and up to rank 256 on LLaMA2-7B, disappearing at r=2048; alpha=2r gives fewer intruder dimensions.
   SRC: LoRA vs Full Fine-tuning: An Illusion of Equivalence | Shuttleworth, Andreas, Torralba, Sharma (MIT), NeurIPS 2025 | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21228v3
[11] (confirmed; conf high) Joint (multi-task) training is the accepted upper bound in LLM continual learning, and rehearsal on model-generated data gets close to it, which supports replaying base-model answers; but general ability still drops measurably even with rehearsal. Related: warm-started networks generalise worse than freshly initialised ones at similar training loss, another reason to restart from base.
   EV: Llama-2-7b, 10 SuperNI tasks: joint training AR 64.69; no rehearsal AR 17.33, BWT -53.64; 1% real-data rehearsal AR 60.64, BWT -5.69; self-synthesized rehearsal AR 63.23, BWT -1.56. Alpaca-7b general ability: MMLU 41.0 before, 34.1 without rehearsal, 37.1 with SSR. Ash and Adams (arxiv.org/abs/1910.08475) report worse generalisation from warm starts.
   SRC: Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in Large Language Models with Self-Synthesized Rehearsal | Huang et al., ACL 2024 | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01244v2
[12] (confirmed; conf high) Critique 1 (most likely disappointment): the parametric path adds little over retrieval alone. In controlled comparisons RAG beats fine-tuning for new facts, and fine-tuning plus RAG is often no better, sometimes worse, than RAG alone. RAG's margin is largest for rare facts, which is what a personal fact log mostly contains.
   EV: Current-events accuracy, base / base+RAG / fine-tuned on paraphrases / fine-tuned+RAG: Mistral 7B 0.481 / 0.875 / 0.588 / 0.830; Llama2 7B 0.353 / 0.585 / 0.392 / 0.520; Orca2 7B 0.456 / 0.876 / 0.566 / 0.826. Accuracy rose monotonically with number of paraphrases (10 per chunk). Soudani et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432): RAG surpasses fine-tuning by a large margin for the least popular knowledge.
   SRC: Fine-Tuning or Retrieval? Comparing Knowledge Injection in LLMs | Ovadia, Brief, Mishaeli, Elisha (Microsoft), EMNLP 2024 | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05934v3
[13] (confirmed; conf high) At realistic scale (500K fact updates) retrieval over the stored edits beats model-editing methods and stacked LoRA fine-tuning; stacked LoRA declines as updates accumulate and merging adapters helps only late. Retrieval's own weak spot is multi-hop questions, which is the niche where a parametric path would have to prove itself.
   EV: WikiBigEdit: 502,382 QA pairs, 8 timesteps, five 7-8B models. RAG averaged 78.64% on rephrased questions and 66.16% on persona rephrasings; only about 10% of multi-hop samples retrieved both facts; inference time at most doubles. ROME/MEMIT collapsed within a few hundred updates; WISE decayed to pre-update levels within about 10K; LoRA-Merge overtook plain LoRA fine-tuning after roughly 100K edits.
   SRC: WikiBigEdit: Understanding the Limits of Lifelong Knowledge Editing in LLMs | Thede, Roth, Bethge, Akata, Hartvigsen, ICML 2025 | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05683v2
[14] (confirmed; conf high) Critique 2: fine-tuning on the log as plain QA rows yields shallow knowledge. Training on raw text can fail to help at all; a fact needs many diverse restatements to become usable; and even then the gain on top of retrieval is small. The log should therefore store source statements and the pipeline should synthesise varied forms (paraphrases, implications, reversals) at training time, multiplying training cost.
   EV: Closed-book QuALITY accuracy on Llama 3 8B: base 39.49%, raw continued pretraining 38.15%, rephrase 43.08%, EntiGraph synthetic 56.42% using about 600M synthetic tokens from a 1.3M-token corpus. With RAG: base 60.35% vs EntiGraph 62.73%. Abstract says models need hundreds to thousands of diverse representations of a fact. Replay of generic pretraining text at rate 0.1 was used against forgetting.
   SRC: Synthetic continued pretraining | Yang, Band, Li, Candes, Hashimoto (Stanford), ICLR 2025 | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07431v1
[15] (confirmed; conf high) Fine-tuned facts generalise less flexibly than the same facts placed in context: fine-tuning scores near zero on reversed questions while in-context learning is near ceiling, unless the training set is augmented with inferences. Without augmentation, knowledge can be memorised yet not extractable. The founder's test (one unseen rewording of a trained question) does not probe reversal or multi-hop use.
   EV: Gemini 1.5 Flash fine-tuned on synthetic facts: near-zero accuracy on the reversal-curse dataset vs near-ceiling in context; augmented fine-tuning closes the gap (t > 149). Allen-Zhu and Li (arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316): without augmentation, 0% extraction accuracy regardless of later instruction fine-tuning. SEAL on Qwen2.5-7B (arxiv.org/html/2506.10943): training on the passage alone 33.5% vs base 32.7%; with synthesised implications 39.7-47.0%.
   SRC: On the generalization of language models from in-context learning and finetuning: a controlled study | Lampinen et al. (Google DeepMind) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661v3
[16] (partly_correct; conf high) Critique 3: retrieved context and fine-tuned weights will conflict whenever a fact changes between retrains, and resolution is unreliable. Fine-tuning can reduce context reliance, models show confirmation bias toward their parametric answer, and they also over-trust wrong context. The design needs an explicit conflict test (retrieved value differs from adapter-trained value) in the gate.
   EV: Context-parametric inversion: Llama2-7B counterfactual context reliance rose from 30% to 60% early in instruction tuning then fell to about 35%; prompting to follow context did not stop the drop. ClashEval (arxiv.org/html/2404.10198v2): 1,294 questions, models adopted incorrect retrieved content over a correct prior more than 60% of the time; best model 74.3% accurate under conflict; adoption is likelier when the prior is low-confidence. Xie et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300): strong confirmation bias when evidence partly matches memory. Survey (arxiv.org/html/2403.08319): no definitive rule for which source wins.
   SRC: Context-Parametric Inversion: Why Instruction Finetuning Can Worsen Context Reliance | Goyal, Baek, Kolter, Raghunathan (CMU), ICLR 2025 | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10796v3
   CORRECTION: The numbers and the survey quote are accurate, but two scopes are narrower than stated. The context-parametric inversion result (30% to 60% to about 35% on Llama2-7B) is about instruction fine-tuning on generic instruction sets, not fine-tuning on facts, so 'fine-tuning can reduce context reliance' for a fact adapter is an extrapolation. The ClashEval 'over 60%' figure is the abstract's headline for GPT-4o on the subset where only the prior was correct (0.608); GPT-4o's overall context bias in the paper is 0.304. Claude Opus at 74.3% and the confidence relationship are as stated. The paper states 1,294 questions, though its per-dataset table sums to 1,278.
[17] (confirmed; conf high) Adapters do not survive base-model upgrades; the data log and the evaluation set do. Published adapter-transfer methods work by regenerating data and retraining on the new base, which is what an append-only log gives directly. A base upgrade also causes per-item regressions even when average accuracy rises, so the gate should track negative flips, not only totals.
   EV: Trans-LoRA abstract: LoRA parameters are specific to the base model and must all be retrained when it is replaced; transfer is done via synthetic data and reported as lossless across Llama and Gemma. MUSCLE (arxiv.org/html/2407.09435v2): Llama 1 to Llama 2 on HellaSwag, accuracy 72.74 to 72.91 yet 10.27% of items flipped from right to wrong; some updates exceed 60% flips; a compatibility adapter cut flips by up to 40%.
   SRC: Trans-LoRA: towards data-free Transferable Parameter Efficient Finetuning | Wang et al. (MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab), NeurIPS 2024; Echterhoff et al. (Apple, UCSD) for MUSCLE | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17258
[18] (confirmed; conf medium) Evaluation-gated promotion is established MLOps practice with tool support: a candidate is compared with the current baseline and promoted only if it clears absolute thresholds and minimum change against the baseline. For this design the gate needs three metric families (new facts, old facts, general) plus a per-item regression rate, and a held-out gate set the founder does not tune on.
   EV: MLflow evaluate accepts validation_thresholds (MetricThreshold with threshold, min_absolute_change, min_relative_change, greater_is_better) and a baseline_model against which the candidate is compared. Google Cloud's MLOps architecture page could not be opened from this environment, so it is not cited.
   SRC: MLflow Python API: mlflow.models (MetricThreshold, evaluate) | MLflow project | 2024 | https://mlflow.org/docs/2.16.2/python_api/mlflow.models.html
[19] (partly_correct; conf high) Critique 4: the current evaluation cannot support promotion decisions. Scores without standard errors, tiny probes, lexical matching and single phrasings are each documented sources of wrong conclusions. By my calculation, 25/25 on a 25-item probe is still compatible with a true rate near 86% (95% Clopper-Pearson lower bound), and 11/25 vs 15/25 is not a significant difference; about 500 paired items are needed to resolve drops of 2-3 points.
   EV: Miller: evaluations should be treated as experiments with questions drawn from a super-population, with formulas for standard errors and paired model comparison. Kamalloo et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2305.06984): more than 50% of lexical-match failures were semantically equivalent answers; one LLM's score rose by nearly 60% under human evaluation. Sclar et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324): prompt formatting alone moved accuracy by up to 76 points on LLaMA-2-13B. Yang et al. (arxiv.org/abs/2502.11177): model-editing success was 38.5% under realistic decoding vs 96.8% reported, and methods failed drastically at 1,000 sequential edits.
   SRC: Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations | Evan Miller (Anthropic) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640
   CORRECTION: All four cited sources say what is claimed (Miller's abstract gives the super-population framing and formulas for comparing two models; I did not open the full text for the paired formula). The researcher's own arithmetic is right on two points: 25/25 has a two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson lower bound of 86.3%, and 11/25 vs 15/25 is not significant. The '500 paired items resolve 2-3 point drops' figure is optimistic. With 500 paired items and about 10% of items changing between two adapters, the standard error of the difference is about 1.4 points, so a 3-point drop is borderline and a 2-point drop is not resolved; it only works if under 5% of items change. Unpaired, 500 items give a standard error of about 2.5 points on the difference. Resolving 2 points needs roughly 1,000 or more items.

[22] (partly_correct; conf medium) Critique 5 (cheaper alternatives): plain files or in-context history are strong baselines that dedicated memory machinery often fails to beat, and at the founder's current scale (100 facts, a few thousand tokens) the whole log fits in the prompt. The parametric path must be compared against 'all facts in context' and 'grep/retrieval over the log', not only against naive fine-tuning.

   CORRECTION: All four sources exist and the Letta figures (74.0% vs Mem0's reported 68.5%, 12 Aug 2025) and Li et al. are as stated. Two are worded more strongly than the abstracts. [23] (partly_correct; conf medium) Whether adaptation belongs in weights or in external scaffolding depends on the kind of change: a 2026 comparison finds prompt-based methods adapt fast but degrade on later tasks, distillation-style weight updates accumulate knowledge stably but struggle to revise outdated facts, and context compression adds efficiency without learning ability. This supports the hybrid split (retrieval for recent, weights for consolidated) but flags fact revision as the weak point of the weight path.
   EV: Abstract compares GEPA and ACE (prompt), SFT and SDFT (supervised), GRPO and SDPO (RL), Cartridges and in-place TTT (compression) on benchmarks recast as sequential streams; no single method wins across change types. Abstract-level evidence only; numbers not extracted.
   SRC: When Does Continual Learning Require Learning | Harrington, Saxena, Murphy, Borovykh, Yun, Kamath, Kyi, Darrell, Malik, Bai (UC Berkeley) | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07847
   CORRECTION: The abstract matches the three quoted conclusions, but the finding omits the fourth: online reinforcement learning adapted most effectively to knowledge updates, though it is sensitive to noisy rewards. So the weight path as a whole is not shown to be weak at revising facts, only distillation-style updates. The paper does not test a retrieval arm, so 'supports the hybrid split (retrieval for recent, weights for consolidated)' is the researcher's inference.
--- OVERSTATEMENTS
 * The summary attaches '0.979 retention, 0.947 on paraphrases' to 'a 2026 preprint on Qwen2.5-1.5B'. Those are Llama-3.2-1B numbers; Qwen2.5-1.5B scored 0.984 and 0.868. They are multiple-choice accuracies on one Wikidata domain dominated by one relation, from a single-author v1 preprint.
 * The summary says cumulative replay came 'with reduced accuracy on an untouched control set'. The paper reports no base-model value for that control set, as the researcher's own finding 5 admits. Only 'stability falls as rank rises on Llama-1B' is supported; on Qwen the two ranks differ by about one point.
 * 'LoRA rank is not the binding limit for memorising thousands of facts' rests on one paper that tested only rank 1 on an 8B model. In the same table reliability fell to 0.48 at 3,000 facts once known-fact rows were mixed in, and a 2026 rank sweep (arXiv 2603.01097) finds lower ranks saturate earlier. The summary also asserts the opposite in finding 7 and the 'sweep rank' implication (rank 8 too low). The two positions are not reconciled.
 * 'Plan replay volume that scales with the number of facts' is presented as the fix for general-knowledge loss. The cited LoRA-packing paper shows MMLU still dropping to 0.554 with known-fact mixing, and SSR shows MMLU 37.1 vs 41.0 with rehearsal. No cited source shows scaled replay holds general ability at thousands of facts on a small model.
 * 'Retraining from the base model instead of stacking is supported' draws on evidence from different settings: RoBERTa-base on six classification tasks with each adapter merged and a new one initialised (not one adapter carried forward), and Ash and Adams on warm-starting networks trained from scratch. No cited source compares from-base cumulative LoRA with a carried-forward adapter on fact injection, and finding 5's paper does not say which it used. It is a reasonable hypothesis to test, not an established result.
 * 'The parametric path adds little over retrieval alone' and 'fine-tuning on top of retrieval gained about 2 points in the best-documented case' generalise from Ovadia (multiple-choice, 7B models, closed-book-trained adapters) and EntiGraph (QuALITY, 8B). EvoWiki reports synergy between RAG and continual learning, and RAFT shows gains when the model is fine-tuned with retrieved context. None of the evidence is on a 1.7B model, where retrieval quality and context use may differ.
 * 'RAG's margin is largest for rare facts, which is what a personal fact log mostly contains' transfers Soudani's popularity result on Wikipedia entities to invented facts; that is an inference.
 * The implication that the adapter should earn promotion on 'two-hop questions' assumes fine-tuned synthetic facts can be composed. The two-hop literature (arXiv 2411.16353) finds they cannot without step-by-step reasoning when both facts are synthetic. WikiBigEdit shows RAG is weak at multi-hop; it does not show fine-tuning is strong at it.
 * 'EvolvingQA shows LoRA does not separate them' is true but at floor level: closed-book exact match is 2 to 3% for every parametric method on T5-large, from a single run. It shows the methods barely learned the facts at all, which is weak evidence about how a modern LoRA that reaches 25/25 would handle changed facts.
 * 'Fine-tuning can lower context reliance', used to justify the conflict suite, comes from instruction fine-tuning on generic instruction data, not fact adapters. The conflict suite is sensible, but the direction of the effect for this design is unknown.
 * The claim that 25 items, keyword match, one seed and endpoint-only scores 'are each named failure modes in the evaluation literature' is fair in spirit, but the cited papers cover lexical matching in open-domain QA, prompt-format sensitivity in few-shot classification, and teacher forcing in model editing. None examines keyword matching on short invented-fact answers, where matching is much less ambiguous than on NQ-open.
 * The '500+ item' probe size is presented as sufficient. By my calculation 500 paired items resolve roughly 3 to 4 point drops, not 2; and for 50-fact rounds or 50 changed facts the per-slice intervals stay wide (about plus or minus 10 to 14 points), so per-round and updated-vs-outdated scores in the proposed experiment will still be noisy.
 * 'Budget for the training set being 10x or more the log size' is given as a planning number. Ovadia used 10 paraphrases, but EntiGraph used about 600M synthetic tokens for a 1.3M-token corpus (roughly 460x) and its abstract speaks of hundreds to thousands of representations per fact. 10x is a lower bound from one source.
 * 'Do not invest in adapter-transfer tricks' is stronger than Trans-LoRA supports: that paper reports its transfer as lossless. The accurate statement is that the log makes such tricks unnecessary, not that they fail.
 * 'TRACE shows narrow fact fine-tuning damages unrelated abilities' overstates; TRACE trains on eight broad task datasets. The founder's own results (general probe 25 to 11-15 of 25) are better direct evidence than TRACE.
 * The MLflow citation establishes that threshold-plus-baseline gating has tool support. The 'three metric families, per-item regression rate, held-out gate set' design and the warning about overfitting the gate are the researcher's recommendations with no source cited for adaptive overfitting.
 * Finding 23 and the related implication call fact revision 'the weak point of the weight path'. The cited abstract says that of distillation-style methods only, and says online RL handled knowledge updates best.
 * The founder's general probe consists of questions the base model already answers, so it can only register losses. The summary treats the general delta as a clean measure and does not flag this one-directional design.
 * The fact-check of findings 5, 6, 10 and 13 relied on long HTML pages that were read only up to the first 100,000 characters; numbers quoted were found, but later sections and appendices were not read.
--- MISSING WORK
 + Understanding LoRA as Knowledge Memory: An Empirical Analysis (Back et al., KAIST / Samsung SDS, arXiv 2603.01097, Mar 2026; search results list it as ICML 2026). Sweeps LoRA rank 2 to 1024 and knowledge load, compares synthetic data formats, multi-LoRA routing and merging, and LoRA combined with in-context or RAG, on models including the Qwen3 family from 0.6B to 14B. — This is a closer analogue than finding 5: it uses Qwen3 itself and asks the exact design question. It finds each adapter has finite capacity with lower ranks saturating earlier, which contradicts 'rank is not the binding limit'. QA-format synthetic data beat raw text. Gains from base size were small between 1.7B and 8B. Routed multi-LoRA can fall below a single adapter. The authors conclude LoRA is a complement to RAG and in-context, rarely a standalone solution. [https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01097v1]
 + Lessons from Studying Two-Hop Latent Reasoning (Balesni, Korbak, Evans; arXiv 2411.16353, originally 'The Two-Hop Curse', v4 Nov 2025). Models fine-tuned on synthetic facts fail two-hop questions without chain-of-thought when both facts are synthetic and learned separately; they can succeed when one fact is natural. — A negative result against the proposed promotion criterion. The researcher wants the adapter to earn its place on two-hop questions over synthetic facts, which is the condition this paper finds fails. The two-hop test must separate with and without step-by-step reasoning, and synthetic-plus-synthetic from synthetic-plus-known hops, or the adapter arm will fail for a known reason. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353]
 + Position: Don't Use the CLT in LLM Evals With Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints (Bowyer, Aitchison, Ivanova; arXiv 2503.01747, ICML 2025). — Directly qualifies the Miller-based advice. Standard-error formulas based on the central limit theorem understate uncertainty on small probes, which is the founder's situation (25 items, 10 per town). Supports exact or Bayesian intervals for the current data and for per-round slices even after the probe grows to 500. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747]
 + Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations? (Gekhman et al., arXiv 2405.05904, EMNLP 2024). — The foundational negative result behind finding 6, not cited. Facts unknown to the model are learned more slowly than known ones, and once learned they linearly increase the tendency to hallucinate. This predicts the founder's 'capital of Japan is Canberra' errors and argues for early stopping and a hallucination or refusal probe in the gate. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904]
 + Continual Memorization of Factoids in Language Models (Chen, Geng, Bhaskar, Friedman, Chen; Princeton, arXiv 2411.07175, revised Feb 2025). Proposes REMIX: mixing random word sequences or generic pretraining text into each stage. — Same problem as the founder's experiment 2 (factoids forgotten over later training stages). Reports that generic or random-data mixing outperforms replay methods at recovering from severe forgetting, a cheap alternative arm to cumulative replay that the proposed five-arm experiment omits. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07175]
 + Forgetting in Language Models: Capacity, Optimization, and Self-Generated Replay (Marek, Cho, Qiu, Chunara, Izmailov, Wilson; arXiv 2605.26097, May 2026). — A 2026 result on the mechanism the design leans on. Self-generated replay nearly eliminates forgetting without stored examples, lower learning rates reduce forgetting at the cost of many more steps, and forgetting persists when the model is near capacity. Supports base-generated replay but warns a small, saturated model may be the hard case. Search results describe the experiments as small-scale, so transfer to Qwen3 1.7B is untested. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26097]
 + Rethinking LoRA Memory Through the Lens of KV Cache Compression (Zuo, Wang, Jurayj, Fleshman, Van Durme; arXiv 2606.05698, Jun 2026). — A direct 2026 test of adapter plus context. A document LoRA adds little when the context is mostly present and recovers 13 to 21 ROUGE-L points only when context is removed; QA-style supervision beats raw-text training. Strengthens critique 1 and says where the adapter can pay off: when the context budget is the constraint. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05698]
 + EvoWiki: Evaluating LLMs on Evolving Knowledge (Tang et al., arXiv 2412.13582, ACL 2025). Tests RAG and continual learning on Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B over stable, evolved and uncharted knowledge. — Counter-evidence to 'the parametric path adds little over retrieval'. The abstract reports a synergistic effect between RAG and continual learning on evolving knowledge. The researcher cites only cases where fine-tuning plus RAG was flat or worse. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13582]
 + RAFT: Adapting Language Model to Domain Specific RAG (Zhang et al., arXiv 2403.10131, 2024). Fine-tunes the model with retrieved documents including distractors. — Shows the fine-tuned-plus-RAG arm depends on how the adapter is trained. Ovadia's fine-tuned+RAG models were trained closed-book and did worse with RAG; RAFT trains with retrieved context and reports consistent gains on PubMed, HotpotQA and Gorilla. The proposed 'LoRA plus retrieval' arm should include a retrieval-aware training variant before concluding the combination does not help. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10131]
 + Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning (Shenfeld, Damani, Hübotter, Agrawal; arXiv 2601.19897, Jan 2026; search results list it in PMLR 306). — This is the SDFT method named in finding 23 but not examined. The model conditioned on the new material in context teaches its own weights, and the abstract reports higher new-task accuracy and much less forgetting than plain supervised fine-tuning on knowledge acquisition tasks. It is a consolidation route from retrieval to weights that the five-arm experiment does not include. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897]
 + How new data permeates LLM knowledge and how to dilute it (Sun et al., Google DeepMind, arXiv 2504.09522, ICLR 2025). 1,320-sample 'Outlandish' dataset measuring priming. — Explains the founder's leak ('the Red Planet is Velmora'): a newly learned fact bleeds into unrelated contexts, predictable from the keyword's prior probability. Two mitigations cut it by 50 to 95%. The gate needs a leak probe, which the researcher's metric list lacks. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09522]
 + Knowledge-Instruct: Effective Continual Pre-training from Limited Data using Instructions (Ovadia, Brief, Lemberg, Sheetrit; arXiv 2504.05571, Apr 2025). — A follow-up by the authors of the finding-12 paper that partly revises its pessimism. Instruction-style synthetic data from a small corpus gives better factual memorisation with limited forgetting and is meant to be used alongside retrieval. Relevant to the 'do not train on bare QA rows' implication; the abstract makes no head-to-head claim against RAG. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05571]
